# Halo — Vadodara Market Research (Desk / Secondary)

> **Method note.** This is *secondary* market research from public sources
> (market reports, news, competitor pages, consumer surveys). It is **not**
> Reddit scraping and contains **no personal data**. It sits one layer above
> the Reddit Research Plan (`reddit-research-plan.md`): this maps the market and
> the gaps; the Reddit study will validate them in locals' own words.
> Figures are India-wide unless a Vadodara-specific source is cited.
> _Compiled June 2026._

---

## 1. What we're building (restated for grounding)

Halo is a **Vadodara-only local discovery layer built on trust**. Its thesis,
from the product itself: *"Vadodara doesn't have a shortage of talent. It has a
trust and clarity problem."* Differentiators baked into the product:

- **Halo Local Score** — reputation built from *real Barodian reviews* after
  real jobs, not self-promotion or bought ratings.
- **No commission** — providers set their own price; Halo takes 0%.
- **AI matching** — "tell Halo what you need," it surfaces who Barodians
  actually trust, same area / similar price.
- **Low-friction for non-marketers** — "you don't have to fight the algorithm
  or become a marketer."

The market question this research answers: *Is the trust-and-clarity gap real
and large in Vadodara, and do the incumbents leave it open?* Short answer from
the evidence below: **yes, on both counts.**

---

## 2. Market context — is the opportunity real?

**Vadodara is a credible tier-2 launch market.**
- 3rd largest city in Gujarat; metro population ~3.5M (Halo's own figure),
  industrial + educational hub that pulls in out-of-towners → steady,
  recurring demand for home/maintenance services from people *without* local
  word-of-mouth networks. [investindia, plumint]
- Consistently ranks top-10 on India's Ease of Living index and is named among
  the fastest-emerging tier-2 cities; McKinsey projects 18 tier-2 cities to
  generate ~$2T by 2030 (up from $690B in 2023). [tradebrains, investindia]

**Digital adoption is past the tipping point.**
- India: ~806M internet users (55% penetration) in early 2025; ~85% of
  households own at least one smartphone; UPI > 10B txns/month — i.e. the
  "find + pay on your phone" behavior Halo assumes is now mainstream.
  [DataReportal, Statista]
- Tier-2/3 cities are the *fastest-growing* digital segment: 56% of
  e-commerce shoppers in FY24 → projected 64% by FY30; >58% of tier-2/3
  consumers expected to adopt digital hyperlocal platforms by 2026. [makreo]

**Demand for the exact categories Halo serves is established locally.**
- Urban Company/UrbanClap explicitly lists Vadodara services: salon-at-home,
  electrician, plumber, carpenter, appliance repair, AC, deep cleaning.
  Multiple platforms now promise 45–90 min doorstep response in the city —
  evidence the category is live and competitive, not nascent. [urbancompany,
  franchiseindia, stylespeak]

> **Takeaway:** the market exists, is digitizing fast, and a key demand source
> (newcomers without local networks) is structurally underserved by
> word-of-mouth — Halo's natural wedge.

## 3. The incumbents and why they leave the gap open

| Player | Model | What it does well | The gap it leaves (Halo's opening) |
|--------|-------|-------------------|-------------------------------------|
| **Urban Company / UrbanClap** | Managed marketplace, **up to 25% commission**, pay-to-work for partners | Standardized, fast doorstep service; brand trust | High commission → higher prices; **provider hostility** (training/onboarding/subscription fees ~₹50k, ID-blocking, surge pressure, privacy complaints, worker protests, Al Jazeera coverage); standardized SKUs squeeze out long-tail/independent local talent. Halo: **0% commission, provider sets price, "claim your spot."** |
| **JustDial** | Paid listings + leads directory | Huge breadth | **Trust collapse** — fake/irrelevant numbers, unauthorized billing, low-quality paid leads, Trustpilot fake-review removals. Listings ≠ trust. Halo: **score from real post-job reviews, no paid placement.** |
| **Sulekha** | Lead-gen for providers | Breadth of categories | Aggressive sales, fake/junk leads (₹60k-for-nothing complaints), spam/robocalls, data-privacy concerns. Halo: **demand finds the provider; no lead farming.** |
| **Google / "near me" + Maps** | Search + reviews | Default first stop | Nearly half of searches have local intent and "near me" grew 5x, but **infra is "broken"** for local services; reviews gameable; no booking/trust layer for independents. Halo: **curated local trust + direct reach.** |
| **WhatsApp / word-of-mouth (status quo)** | Social referral | Highest trust source | Doesn't scale; **fails newcomers and long-tail needs**; no way to adjudicate conflicting names. Halo: **systematizes social proof into a portable Local Score.** |

**Why this matters:** the incumbents split into two camps, and *both* leave
Halo's middle open —
1. **High-trust but doesn't scale** (word-of-mouth/WhatsApp).
2. **Scales but low-trust** (directories, lead-gen, gameable reviews) or
   **scales but extractive** (25% commission, provider-hostile).

Halo's bet is the unoccupied quadrant: **scalable *and* trusted *and*
provider-friendly.**

## 4. The gaps, with evidence (the heart of the research)

### Gap 1 — The trust deficit is documented, not anecdotal
- **68%** of Indian consumers have encountered fake reviews; **32%** say fake
  reviews influenced a purchase (LocalCircles). [ratinggrow/outlook]
- **94%** say a single negative review made them avoid a business — reputation
  is decisive, yet the rating systems are gamed. [ratinggrow]
- Even referrals now trigger a "check reviews first" step — but the reviews
  people check are the *least* trusted because of manipulation. The result:
  **a trust vacuum no current platform fills.** → *Directly validates Halo's
  Local Score as a defensible wedge.*

### Gap 2 — Local discovery is structurally "broken," worst in tier-2
- ~**40%+** of small local businesses lack meaningful online presence; most
  who try find digital marketing "too expensive or too complex." [Tribune]
- Genuinely good providers stay invisible because they don't "game algorithms
  or buy reviews"; directories haven't adapted; social isn't built for
  real-time service discovery. → *Validates Halo's "don't become a marketer"
  + AI-matching promise.*
- "In tier-2/3 cities... the mismatch between consumer demand and digital
  supply is glaring." → *Validates the Vadodara-first focus specifically.*

### Gap 3 — Incumbent economics squeeze both sides
- Up to **25% commission** + pay-to-work fees mean providers earn less and
  customers pay more; worker protests and ID-blocking show provider trust in
  the platform is itself broken. → *Validates Halo's 0-commission,
  provider-owned-profile model as a recruiting and pricing advantage.*

### Gap 4 — Demand without a network (the newcomer problem)
- Vadodara's industrial/education pull means a large floating population with
  recurring home-service needs and **no local word-of-mouth** to draw on —
  exactly the user for whom "who do I trust?" is unanswered today. → *A
  concrete, addressable beachhead segment for launch.*

### Gap 5 — Adjudication, not discovery
- Synthesis across the above: in a digitized city the problem has shifted from
  *"I can't find anyone"* to *"I can find ten names and can't tell who's good."*
  Halo's own copy nails this ("You can find ten names..."). The market data
  (fake reviews, broken directories) confirms the bottleneck is **choosing,
  not searching.** → *This is the single most important hypothesis for the
  Reddit study to confirm or refute (it maps to pain code P2 in the plan).*

## 6. Strategic implications for Halo

1. **Lead with trust, not breadth.** The market is drowning in listings and
   starved of trust. Halo wins on *adjudication* (the Local Score), not on
   having the most names.
2. **Recruit providers on economics + dignity.** "0% commission, you own your
   profile, you set your price" is a sharp contrast to the 25%-commission,
   pay-to-work, ID-blocking incumbent. Provider supply is the harder side of
   the marketplace — this is the recruiting message.
3. **Target the no-network newcomer first.** Highest pain, clearest value,
   recurring need.
4. **Protect the Local Score's integrity above all.** The entire moat collapses
   if reviews become gameable like everyone else's. Verified-after-real-job is
   the product's crown jewel — treat review integrity as a core, not a feature.
5. **Stay Vadodara-deep, not India-wide.** The gap is "glaring" precisely
   *because* it's local; depth (real Barodian reviews, local pricing, area
   knowledge) is the defensibility a national directory can't replicate.
